Going uphill can be demanding, especially on a blistering summer day with a steep incline. What once felt like a smooth glide can suddenly become a tough, almost insurmountable task.

To address this, I've created a pair of rollerblades equipped with an electric wheel, a compact battery, accelerometers, and a CPU. These rollerblades have sensors to detect the slope, the active leg, the angle of push, and which wheel is gaining traction. When needed, the CPU activates a 5-10% boost in speed based on the incline. Additionally, a pressure sensor in the boots allows the CPU to monitor and prevent backward slipping.

When designing these rollerblades, I prioritized safety, ease of use, and the gear's total weight. The electric wheel with built-in battery adds about 300g to each boot, and I plan to recharge the batteries during downhill rides to offset this extra weight. The motor's assistance is gentle and timed to coincide with the skater's own push.

To simplify the design, I aim to eliminate wires and charging ports, exploring alternatives like wireless charging, easy battery swaps, and methods to reduce weight. There is an optional wheel for recovering energy and braking downhill. Additionally, LEDs are used for inter-CPU communication, doubling as safety illumination. I'm also considering a configuration feature and a gradual introduction mode covering the first 3-5km, allowing the system to adapt to the user’s skill level.

Higher average speed requires larger, softer wheels for smoother ride and better grip.

This project draws inspiration from products like RazErblades and off-road electric rollerblades such as Skopinski and Powerslide Metropolis SUV. I’m also looking at innovations like Moonwalkers by Shift Robotics, designed for enhanced mobility.

## Same technology in fall-prevention shoes

The same building blocks (pressure sensors in the sole, an accelerometer, a small CPU and a motorized wheel) can be used for a very different purpose: keeping elderly people on their feet. The skate CPU already detects which leg is loaded, the push angle and backward slipping. A slip or a loss of balance looks the same to the sensors, so the shoe can do more than assist: it can catch the wearer.

The idea: a regular-looking shoe with a retractable motorized wheel or roller under the sole. When the CPU detects the onset of a fall (sudden shift of the pressure center, abnormal acceleration of the foot, a slip), the motor moves the foot in the direction that brings it back under the center of mass, the same way a person takes a corrective step. Between events the system only monitors, so the shoe works as a gait tracker (stride variability, sway, asymmetry), which can warn about rising fall risk early. Wireless charging and energy recovery from walking, as in the skates, keep the weight and maintenance down. The assistance should be gentle and have a break-in mode, like the skates, to avoid causing a fall by a false trigger.

Open questions: false positives (a shoe that moves the foot unexpectedly is a hazard), response time (a fall has to be detected within roughly 100-200 ms), forward and sideways falls (not only backward), battery life, and regulation (probably a medical device, FDA 510(k) or equivalent).

Prior art and related work. Ordered by how directly the product acts on a fall as it happens. Claims marked "(vendor)" come from the company's own material, not an independent review.

**Acts in real time during the fall**

*  **B-Shoe** (B-Shoe Technologies, Haifa) - shoe with a pressure sensor, motor, battery and microprocessor. When the wearer starts to fall, the motor rolls the foot backward until balance is regained. Crowdfunded, development began in 2008, a market launch was targeted for 2016. No evidence it shipped: a 2020 article still called it a prototype, the Startup Nation Central listing has no data, and an inventor interview noted that effectiveness against falls was not yet known. It only addresses backward falls; FDA 510(k) clearance was expected to be needed. This is the closest prior art to this idea. [Times of Israel](https://www.timesofisrael.com/with-b-shoes-elderly-wont-fall-as-much-company-hopes/), [Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/innovation/auto-balancing-b-shoe-could-stop-the-elderly-from-falling), [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/crowdfunded-smart-shoe-aims-prevent-falls-stepping-you), [NoCamels](https://nocamels.com/2014/05/israeli-fall-prevention-motorized-shoe-is-a-step-in-the-right-direction/), [AgedPlus 2020](https://agedplus.co.nz/2020/11/24/anti-fall-shoes-in-development/), [Startup Nation Central](https://finder.startupnationcentral.org/company_page/b-shoe-technologies)
*  **Wearable robotic slip-fall prevention (research)** - exoskeleton-type devices detect balance loss in real time and apply a corrective torque. The Active Pelvis Orthosis compares hip angles with an adaptive-oscillator prediction, stays transparent during normal walking and adds hip torque on balance loss; tested on 8 elderly people and 2 transfemoral amputees. Knee devices (ROKAD, and a knee device that extends the trailing leg) reduce slip distance. All lab prototypes, none sold. [Nature Sci. Rep.](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep46721), [Frontiers in Robotics and AI 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/robotics-and-ai/articles/10.3389/frobt.2024.1367474/xml), [Knee exoskeleton with unexpected foot slip](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/374458938_Knee_Exoskeleton-Enabled_Balance_Control_of_Human_Walking_Gait_with_Unexpected_Foot_Slip), [Tech Briefs](https://www.techbriefs.com/component/content/article/31689-smart-robotic-exoskeleton-predi)
*  **Active smart shoe concept** - a February 2026 article describes a shoe with inertial and pressure sensors and small actuators that applies a corrective action when it detects a likely trip. No company or developer is named, so treat it as a concept. [Newshub](https://www.newshub.co.uk/health-wellness/2026/02/24/smart-shoe-technology-to-prevent-elderly-falls-and-support-independence/)

**Detects the fall in progress and protects (does not prevent it)**

*  **Tango Belt** (ActiveProtective) - the strongest evidence in this category. 3-D motion sensors recognize a serious hip-impacting fall in progress and the airbag deploys in under 90 ms. FDA De Novo (DEN240021). Pivotal study: 91% relative risk reduction in major hip injury, ED visits down 66%, hospitalizations down 56% (vendor). Compliance in a multicenter study was 67%, no serious device-related adverse events. About 2 lb, single-use airbag. Reviews are scarce: three user ratings on Revain average about 4 of 5 (easy to put on, comfortable; the only complaint is price), and a nursing-journal feasibility report (by the developer) found long-term-care wear above 80% of care plan orders. Named to TIME Best Inventions 2026. [Product](https://www.tangobelt.com/how-it-works), [FDA De Novo file](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN240021.pdf), [Rheumatology Advisor](https://www.rheumatologyadvisor.com/news/fda-clears-smart-belt-to-reduce-the-risk-of-hip-injury-due-to-fall/), [Multicenter study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11690325/), [Nursing journal](https://ojin.nursingworld.org/table-of-contents/volume-26-2021/number-3-september-2021/tango-belt-a-new-smart-hip-protector-solution/), [Revain reviews](https://revain.org/reviews/tango-belt-app), [Aging in Home Technology review](https://aiht.substack.com/p/tango-belt-2b8), [video review "$800 belt"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oTj_EUhkNFM), [TIME 2026 release](http://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/tango-belt-named-to-times-list-of-best-inventions-2026-302910337.html)
*  **Helite Hip'Air / Hip'Guard** - gyroscopes and accelerometers sampled up to 1,000 times per second; detection within 200 ms and inflation 80 ms later, against an average fall of about 0.4 s (vendor). Claimed 90% impact force reduction. A retailer cites University of Grenoble-Alpes validation: 90% of lateral falls detected with 1% false activation, so roughly 1 in 10 lateral falls would not trigger it. About $750-800 at CES 2018, 2.2 lb, battery up to 15 days. No independent fracture-prevention trial found. [New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/helite-hipair-wearable-airbag/52867/), [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/health/high-tech-airbag-could-save-seniors-from-breaking-their-hips), [HuffPost UK](https://www.huffingtonpost.co.uk/entry/helite-hip-mounted-airbag-for-the-eldery_uk_5a534973e4b0efe47eba54e6), [Deccan Chronicle (CES 2018)](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/technology/in-other-news/080118/ces-2018-a-belt-with-sensors-to-protect-your-hip.html), [Helite Australia](https://www.helite.com.au/product-page/hip-safe-senior-airbag)
*  **Hip-Hope** (Hip-Hope Technologies) - smart belt with a multi-sensor system and two airbags over the hips, automatic alerts to contacts, distress button. Beta tested at Sheba Medical Center, 2015 Innovation Award (vendor). A 2016 article said it would be sold in Europe and the US by the end of that year; I found no confirmation it reached the market and no independent reviews. [Company](https://www.hip-hope.com/), [EBV blog](https://blog.ebv.com/hiphope-is-an-airbag-for-your-hips/), [TelecareAware (includes criticism of the category: a frequent faller can fall again before the airbag is reset)](https://telecareaware.com/a-wearable-airbag-belt-to-prevent-hip-fractures-due-to-falls/)
*  **Category warning:** a narrative review found that Helite and Tango had not been rigorously tested for reducing hip injury at the time, and that avoiding false deployments means they will not protect in every real-world fall. A 2025 case report of another commercial hip airbag belt (not Hip-Hope) describes the belt slipping down to the thighs, causing a fall and a distal femur fracture; the trial was halted for a safety review. [Narrative review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10172985/), [2025 case report](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12877-025-05866-0)
*  **Monitor + Quin smart safety shoes** - work shoes with fall detection and emergency alerts, scheduled to launch in autumn 2025. Detects and alerts after the fall, no actuator. [Health & Safety International](https://www.healthandsafetyinternational.com/article/1920707/smart-safety-shoes-step-future-workplace-sos), [Security and Fire Africa](https://securityafricamagazine.com/monitor-reveals-worlds-first-smart-safety-shoes-with-fall-detection-tech/)

**Improves balance continuously (feedback or stimulation), no fall-time action**

*  **Walkasins** (RxFunction) - lower-limb sensory prosthesis: a Receptor Sole in the shoe reads foot pressure and a leg module with four vibrating motors per leg feeds it back to the wearer. For peripheral neuropathy with high fall risk. The walk2Wellness trial reported better Functional Gait Assessment, gait speed and Timed Up & Go after 10 weeks, and fall rates at 26 weeks. I could not confirm the current FDA status; an old press release said it was registered and listed but not yet for sale. [10-week results](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7680959/), [26-week results](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9531134/), [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/researchers-find-short-term-use-of-walkasins-improves-balance-walking-speed-and-lowers-fall-risk-for-peripheral-neuropathy-patients), [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT02115633](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02115633)
*  **NEURVESTA (Neursantys)** - wearable that combines a behind-the-ear balance sensor (Phybrata, 2-minute assessment) with sub-threshold stochastic electrical vestibular stimulation, 3 x 20 min per week for 6 weeks. Piloted in assisted living and neuro-rehab clinics; an earlier RCT with a prototype reported effects lasting up to six months. [Company](https://neursantys.com/), [Senior living study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13015830/), [MassAITC pilot](https://massaitc.org/2026/01/10/b-neurvesta-balance-restoration-neursantys/), [Caring Hands](https://www.chcgivers.com/technology-enhanced-living/neursantys/)
*  **Noisy galvanic vestibular stimulation** - sub-sensory current behind the ears. In 30 elderly participants postural stability improved for over 2 hours after stimulation stopped. A multicenter crossover trial in vestibulopathy found no clear benefit over placebo (-9.3% vs -12.6% velocity). Trials continue (NCT06688578, NCT06846047). I found no FDA-cleared product. [Sci. Rep. 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep37575), [JMIR protocol](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8527374/), [PLOS ONE crossover trial](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0317822)
*  **Vibrating insoles** - Priplata et al., Lancet 2003: subsensory noise (stochastic resonance) reduced sway in all measured variables in the elderly. Later work showed gait variability reduction in elderly fallers and effects in diabetes and stroke. The closest therapeutic device is the SENSOLE 2 insole, tested in a randomized study on older adults with type 2 diabetes. No mainstream consumer product found. [Lancet 2003 (PDF)](http://www.collinslab.mit.edu/s/priplata2003lancet.pdf), [Baltimore Sun](https://www.baltimoresun.com/2003/10/05/study-says-vibrating-shoe-insoles-can-improve-balance-in-elderly/), [SENSOLE 2 RCT](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9076777), [Nature Sci. Rep. active insoles](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-58815-9)
*  **Textured insoles** - mixed evidence and can impair balance in some wearers (no single product; no link found in this pass).

**Monitoring and training only**

*  **Pedisteps / VRsteps** - Pedisol insoles ($349) or shoes ($499) with 40 pressure sensors per insole, app and GaitIQ dashboard with a continuously updated fall risk score. Monitors, does not stimulate. All claims are from the vendor; no independent validation found. [Pedisteps](https://www.vrsteps.io/pedisteps/), [Insoles](https://www.vrsteps.io/product/pedisol-insoles/), [Shoes](https://www.vrsteps.io/product/pedisol-shoes/), [Fall risk blog](https://www.vrsteps.io/blog/smart-insoles-for-gait-monitoring-and-fall-risk/)
*  **GaitBetter** - treadmill with VR and cognitive tasks. Best evidence in the list: the V-TIME randomized trial (Lancet) of VR added to treadmill training for fall risk. The company claims up to an 80% fall reduction (vendor, check against the paper). A 60-patient clinical experience report (no control group) showed TUG time down 10.3%. Recognized by the National Council on Aging (press release). Training in a clinic, not a wearable. [Company](https://www.gaitbetter.com/), [V-TIME RCT](https://www.gaitbetter.com/the-lancet-addition-of-a-non-immersive-virtual-reality-component-to-treadmill-training-to-reduce-fall-risk/), [Clinical experience](https://www.gaitbetter.com/physical-therapy-rehabilitation-treadmill-training-program-with-virtual-reality/), [NCOA recognition](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/national-council-aging-officially-recognizes-gaitbetters-gaitactive-evidence)
*  **Medigait GaitAid** (founded 2008 by Prof. Yoram Baram, Technion) - a "virtual walker": waist sensor detects heel strikes, plays sound cues and moves a checkerboard in goggles. For freezing of gait in Parkinson's, MS and stroke; 20-30% gait improvement claimed in promotional coverage. Status unclear: Startup Nation Central says it ceased operating in June 2017, Crunchbase still lists it. [ISRAEL21c](https://archive.israel21c.org/helping-the-elderly-get-steady-on-their-feet/), [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2009/10/20/208868/senior-momentum), [Product page](http://medigait.com/main/multiple-sclerosis/), [Startup Nation Central](https://finder.startupnationcentral.org/company_page/medigait)
*  **Research smart insoles and shoes** - fall-risk detection above 87% accuracy with a self-powered insole ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1385894726006492)); real-time intelligent shoe for fall detection ([IEEE](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6181633/)); near-fall detection with machine learning ([Sensors 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11902511/)). Useful for the detection algorithm: near-fall detection is the same problem the skate CPU solves.

Gap: pre-impact detection is solved well enough to ship (Tango, Helite: roughly 100-200 ms), but those products only cushion the landing. Products that improve balance (Walkasins, vestibular stimulation, vibrating insoles) work continuously, not at the moment of a stumble. The only product that tried to act on the fall itself with the foot, B-Shoe, appears not to have shipped. The skate platform, already built around slip detection and a motorized wheel, is a natural base. The detection side can reuse the airbag belts' approach and the research on near-fall detection.
